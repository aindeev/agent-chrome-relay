Chrome Relay

Privacy

The Chrome Relay Approve iPhone app, and the relay it uses. Last updated October 12, 2026. (The Chrome extension's policy is here.)

Chrome Relay lets your AI coding agents use your Chrome on your Mac. Chrome Relay Approve is its iPhone app: it lets you approve, with Face ID, what an agent asks for when you are away from the Mac. Both are open source (github.com/aindeev/agent-chrome-relay), so everything below can be checked in the code.

In short

  • We don't collect personal data. There are no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking.
  • What agents ask you to approve (which agent, which site, which account) is encrypted on your Mac so that only your paired iPhone can read it, and the other way round for your answer.
  • A small relay passes those encrypted messages between your Mac and iPhone and wakes the app through Apple's push service. It can't read them, and it deletes them within 12 hours.

On your Mac

Chrome Relay runs on your Mac and keeps its data there: its settings, an audit trail of what agents did in your browser (in ~/.chrome-relay), and its keys and tokens in your Mac's Keychain. If you connect 1Password, logins are read from the vaults you choose, on your Mac, and typed into the sites they belong to; they are never sent anywhere else. None of this is sent to us.

On your iPhone

Chrome Relay Approve keeps on your iPhone: the Macs you paired it with, their keys, and a log of your last 100 approvals. Its signing key is created in your iPhone's Secure Enclave and never leaves it. The app asks for Face ID to sign an approval, and for notifications so it can tell you when an agent is waiting. It has no account, no analytics and no third-party code.

The relay

Your Mac and iPhone can't connect to each other directly, so they meet at a relay: a small service we run on Cloudflare Workers (chrome-relay-push.aindeev.workers.dev). It handles:

  • Encrypted messages between a paired Mac and iPhone: the pairing handshake, requests, answers, and your iPhone's push address. They are encrypted end to end (P-256 key agreement, AES-GCM) with keys only the two devices have; the relay stores the ciphertext, under a random name only the two devices know, until the other side fetches it, for at most 12 hours, then deletes it.
  • Waking your iPhone. When an agent is waiting, your Mac sends the relay your iPhone's push address, and the relay asks Apple's push service to show a fixed notification ("An agent is waiting for your approval"). The relay keeps nothing about this.

Like any web service, the relay and Cloudflare see the IP address and time of each request; Cloudflare may keep these in its logs under its privacy policy. We don't use them to identify you. Apple delivers the notification under its privacy policy.

You don't have to use our relay: pairing with chrome-relay phone pair --server uses any ntfy server instead (your own, for example), and you can run your own copy of the relay from the repository.

What we can't do

We can't see what your agents ask for or which sites they use, can't read your 1Password logins, and can't approve anything: an approval is a signature from your iPhone's Secure Enclave, which your Mac checks.

Children

Chrome Relay is a developer tool and isn't directed at children.
